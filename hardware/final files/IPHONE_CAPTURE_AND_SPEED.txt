IPHONE THREE-TUBE CAPTURE AND MOTOR SPEED

The scanner website now has Capture setup > Three tubes on the rotating base.
This is a manual stop-and-capture workflow. The website does not yet send
commands to the UNO Q or automatically trigger a phone photo after motor DONE.

1. Open the updated scanner website on the iPhone and choose three-tube mode.
2. Fix the phone in landscape, camera at label height. Use diffuse lighting.
   Fit each complete tube and its label within its own third of the image.
   Keep phone position, zoom, tube positions and orientation unchanged.
3. Use Start camera over trusted HTTPS, or Take photo with iPhone. Live capture
   requests the rear camera and high resolution; actual resolution varies by
   device/browser. Landscape native camera photos can be sharper than a stream.
4. Capture a first view while stationary. Check the three cropped previews:
   each must contain exactly one tube's label, in focus, without clipped text.
5. Send n in the UNO Q Monitor, wait for DONE, then take the next photo.
   n advances approximately 45 degrees at each tube. Repeat seven times for
   eight views total, covering one revolution. A final n returns near the
   starting orientation if desired, without taking a ninth photo.
6. Upload tubes as separate samples. Left, middle and right images are grouped
   separately, up to eight views each. Keep tube positions fixed until done.
   If an upload fails, remaining photos are kept and new capture is blocked.
   Check the session before retrying if the connection failed after sending:
   the server may have accepted a request whose response never reached the phone.
7. Review the decoded labels in the existing scanner and confirm each sample.
   Left/middle/right are physical positions, not verified sample identities.

MOTOR SKETCH
Open motor_test/motor_test.ino and upload it to the UNO Q using your existing
working Arduino setup. This file has been host-simulation tested, not compiled
against the installed UNO Q core or uploaded to the physical board here.
Select speed only while idle:
1 = original 4 ms step interval (still the startup default)
2 = moderate 3 ms step interval; test this first
3 = fast 2 ms step interval; experimental under the actual load
n = next 45-degree tube view
j = short motor jog; m = motor turn; t = complete tube turn
s = stop and release; r = reverse the next motion; ? = help

1 - original: about 33.8 seconds per complete tube revolution, including the 0.5-second settling pause.
2 - moderate: about 25.7 seconds per complete tube revolution, including the 0.5-second settling pause.
3 - fast test: about 17.7 seconds per complete tube revolution, including the 0.5-second settling pause.
These are calculated timing estimates, not measured hardware performance.
Eight separate views include repeated acceleration and settling, so a scan
will take longer than one continuous revolution, plus photo/upload time.
Keep the motor at its rated 5 V. Verify ULN2003 driver and supply before wiring.
Try higher speeds unloaded, then with empty tubes. If it clicks, stalls or
fails to return to a reference mark, return to a slower preset. Open-loop step
counts cannot detect slipping. Do not assume a faster command achieved its angle.

The faster presets do not require changing or reprinting gears.
The three tubes still rotate together; independent image records do not mean
independent mechanical rotation. End-to-end iPhone capture and loaded motor
performance still need a physical test.
